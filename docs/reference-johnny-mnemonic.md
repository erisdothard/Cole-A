# Reference analysis — Johnny Mnemonic (1995) Trailer #1

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EFJY_BZ55sI (Rotten Tomatoes Classic Trailers, 2:28). The reference Cole attached to `client-brief.md`.
Frames and contact sheets live in `reference/trailer/` (gitignored — copyrighted footage, reference only; nothing from the film ships on the site).

Only the on-screen computer graphics matter here. The live-action 80% of the trailer is ignored.

## The five useful sequences

| Time | What is on screen | What to take from it |
|------|-------------------|----------------------|
| 0:00–0:04 | Dark data-city, then a white-violet core with orange/teal radial streaks; loose numerals and glyphs orbit the core and fly past camera. Cuts to an eye with a blue monitor reflected in it, then a full white flash. | **The "pulled inside" transition.** Radial zoom-blur tunnel, characters as debris, white-out at the end. | 0:19 / 1:35 | Full-screen HUD. Header `SEARCH > COPYSHOP LAYOUT: FOUND` (also `BUFFER: FOUND`). Cyan line-drawn floorplan in a dark inset panel floating over a royal-blue circuit-trace texture. Gloved VR hands reach into the frame. | **The window language.** Thin cyan strokes, no fills, dark inset panels on a patterned field, tiny labels, numeric noise in the margins, `PATH > OBJECT: STATE` header grammar. |
| 0:27 | `SCAN 2` / `SCAN 3` — small stacked thumbnail panels on a flat saturated blue field beside one large dark viewport holding a rotating wireframe skull. | **Thumbnail rail + main viewport** layout for browsing work. |
| 0:56 / 1:01 | Videophone: cyan 3×4 keypad with `◄ ■ ►` transport keys beside a live video feed, phone number beneath. Then a green/cyan street map with a red crosshair tracking across it on a curved glass CRT set into a grey bezel with screw dots. | **Contact = a videophone keypad.** **Index/map = a targeting display.** Physical bezel around the screen. |

## Palette (sampled by eye from the CG frames — measure before locking)

- Ground: pure black, and a deep royal blue circuit field (~`#0a1e6e` → `#1436a8`)
- Primary line: cyan / teal (~`#35e0d0`), with a paler cyan for secondary strokes
- Neon architecture: violet-white (~`#b9b4ff`) with a blue outer glow
- Alert / title: signal red (~`#d8141c`) — used for the title cards, the crosshair, the one "live" panel
- Scanner: phosphor green (~`#3cff7a`)
- Heat: orange/amber streaks only inside the transition tunnel and the chip core

Notably **not** the green-on-black Matrix look. It is blue-cyan-violet with red as the single interrupt colour. CVZ's own neon-blue logo clip already sits inside this palette.

## Typography

- Title cards (`THE DANGER`, `IN HIS HEAD`): heavy condensed geometric sans, all caps, tight tracking, red on black — with a **diagonal slash cut through the N and the A**. That cut letterform is the most ownable type detail in the reference.
- HUD headers: wide, extended all-caps sans, light weight, generous tracking (`SEARCH > COPYSHOP LAYOUT: FOUND`).
- HUD micro-labels: tiny caps over digit columns; one serif-ish lowercase label (`elevation: coordinates in sector 10`) — the mismatch is part of the period feel.
- Credits block: ultra-condensed red caps.

## Motion and distortion

- Transitions are zoom-blur and white-flash, not fades.
- Everything in cyberspace moves along one-point perspective toward a vanishing point; objects arrive from the centre and pass the camera.
- CRT behaviours: curved glass, scanlines, blue cast, bloom on bright strokes, vertical-line collapse on power-off.
- Pacing is fast-cut, but the HUD screens themselves are static and legible while the background moves — the same split the brief asks for ("immersion vs. usability").

## System-message grammar to adopt

`SEARCH > {THING}: FOUND` · `BUFFER: FOUND` · `SCAN 2` · `{PLACE}: {ROOM}` · `elevation: coordinates in sector 10` · `X (…) Y (…) Z (…)` · "Good morning. This is your wake-up call." (the trailer's first line — a ready-made boot greeting tone).

## Do not copy

The AT&T mark, the film's title lockup, character likenesses, and any frame of footage. This document exists to extract a visual grammar, not assets.
